# C1 conditioning: concatenation vs FiLM vs hypernetwork / generated low-rank updates

Literature scan done 2026-10-26 for the HyperKKL TMLR revision. The question behind it: after equal-budget tuning, a decoder that concatenates the 16-dim input context ties a hypernetwork that generates low-rank decoder updates, on 2-5-state plants at about 140k parameters. Is that surprising given other fields?

Verification key: **V** means the title, authors and venue were checked against a proceedings page, publisher page or mlanthology/arXiv record, and the numbers were read from the paper's full text (ar5iv/arXiv HTML or extracted PDF). **V-abs** means the bibliographic data was verified but the claim comes from the abstract or a secondary summary only. **U** means unverified; do not cite without checking.

## 2. Synthesis

### (1) Is concatenation common and competitive?
Yes, it is common and often competitive. Concatenation is the default in DeepSDF/auto-decoders, MAD (parametric PDEs), data-controlled neural ODEs, VariBAD/RL^2 meta-RL policies, CAVIA, and cVAE/cGAN generators. Several controlled comparisons find it ties or nearly ties a hypernetwork when the context is low-dimensional:
- Rebain et al. (TMLR 2023): concat is about equal to the hypernetwork at every latent size on Tiled-MNIST.
- MetaSDF: about equal in distribution; concat is better out of distribution.
- HyperPINN: the large concat-PINN is within about 20% of HyperPINN at a similar total parameter count.
- Beck et al.: concat is only modestly worse than the best-initialised hypernetwork and beats it on Humanoid.

A second structural point: shift modulation applied at every layer (functa, DINo-style "shift-only" modulation) is algebraically identical to concatenating the latent to the input of every layer (Dumoulin et al. 2018 identity, applied per layer). Functa reports that shift-only "performs just as well" as scale+shift. A large part of the neural-field literature therefore already runs on concatenation-equivalent conditioning under another name. The impression that "no one uses concatenation" is wrong. What is true is that papers proposing a new mechanism rarely tune the concat baseline as carefully as their own method.

### (2) When do FiLM or hypernetworks beat concatenation, and when do they tie?
They tend to win when:
- **The context is high-dimensional relative to width** (N >> k): concat becomes a linear bottleneck (Rebain). Here attention beats both concat and the hypernetwork.
- **The map must change multiplicatively or strongly with the context**: products and "matching" (Dumoulin; Jayakumar), or a periodic/SIREN backbone, where the phase or frequency must move. In pi-GAN, FiLM vs concat FID is 5.15 vs 21.6 with SIREN but only 26.8 vs 32.0 with ReLU+PE. In FiLM/CLEVR, the scale term is worth +1.5 points.
- **Few data per context, adaptation or extrapolation to new contexts, multi-task or continual learning**, where a low-rank/norm-constrained hypothesis space acts as a regulariser: CoDA's adaptation gap is about 5x, HyperPCM's gains are zero-shot, and von Oswald's advantage is in forgetting.
- **Inference-cost constraints**, where a small main net is wanted (HyperPINN). This is a compute argument, not an accuracy one.

They tend to tie when:
- The context is low-dimensional (2-16), varies smoothly, and enters mostly additively.
- The backbone is a ReLU/tanh MLP that is wide relative to the context.
- Plenty of data covers the context distribution, and evaluation is in distribution.
- The budget is matched on *total* trainable parameters and each method's hyperparameters are tuned.

Our regime (2-5 states, 16-dim context, 140k params, equal-budget tuning) sits squarely in the "tie" regime. A tie there agrees with Rebain/MetaSDF/HyperPINN/Beck and is not an anomaly. Galanti & Wolf's separation needs per-context functions that differ structurally and both networks allowed to grow large; neither is likely to hold here.

### (3) Avoidable reasons a hypernetwork can underperform concatenation
1. **Initialisation.** Standard init on the hypernetwork's output layer gives generated weights at the wrong variance (Chang et al. 2020). In meta-RL, a Kaiming-initialised hypernetwork reaches only 12-20% of the concat baseline's return (Beck et al. 2022). Remedies: hyperfan-in/out; Bias-HyperInit (zero output weights, bias = a standard base init). For generated **low-rank updates** the analogue is LoRA-style init: one factor zero, so the delta is 0 at the start and the base decoder trains like the concat model. Check that our HyperKKL implementation does this.
2. **Context-norm proportionality.** With a zero-bias ReLU hypernetwork, the generated parameter scale is proportional to ||c|| (Ortiz et al. 2024). Inputs and learned context encodings with varying norm then cause gradient-scale swings. Remedies: normalise or constant-norm-encode the context; use a residual parametrisation theta = theta0 + h(c); apply LayerNorm on the hypernetwork input.
3. **Optimiser and learning-rate sensitivity.** Default hypernetworks can fail with SGD at all learning rates, while Adam rescues them (Ortiz et al.). The generated factors A(c)B(c) are a product of two learned maps, so the effective step size is second-order in the hypernetwork parameters. A shared learning rate tuned for the concat model is not neutral, and the hypernetwork may need a separate (smaller) output-layer LR or warm-up. Inference from the cited mechanisms; not directly measured in a cited paper.
4. **Regressing too many outputs.** Full-weight generation is ill-conditioned; low-rank generation fixes most of it (HyperLoRA: about 100x lower error). Too high a rank re-introduces the problem, and CoDA finds the best rank = the number of truly varying factors.
5. **Fairness of the comparison.** Most "hypernetwork wins" papers do not tune the concat baseline equally. They often match the *main-net* size rather than the total (HyperPINN), or let concat touch only the first-layer bias (CAVIA-Concat in CoDA). Rebain shows that splitting the latent across layers improves concat by about 4 dB. A properly tuned concat baseline is therefore expected to close much of the published gap.

### Suggested wording for the paper
"Consistent with controlled comparisons in neural fields (Rebain et al., 2023; Sitzmann et al., 2020) and parametric PDEs (de Avila Belbute-Peres et al., 2021), we find that with a low-dimensional context and equal tuning budgets, input concatenation, which is equivalent to a conditional bias (Dumoulin et al., 2018), matches generated low-rank weight updates. Hypernetwork advantages reported elsewhere concentrate in high-dimensional contexts, multiplicative or periodic dependence, and few-shot or out-of-distribution adaptation (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022; Chan et al., 2021), and require hypernetwork-specific initialisation and input normalisation (Chang et al., 2020; Beck et al., 2022; Ortiz et al., 2024)."
