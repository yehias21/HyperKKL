\section{How much information the measurements carry, and when any observer must fail}\label{sec:m4}

\paragraph{Setting.} The sampled plant exactly as the data generator produces it (\texttt{data.integrate}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m4-plant}
x_{k+1}=\Phi(x_k,u_k)+w_k,\qquad y_k=h(x_k)+v_k,\qquad w_k\sim\mathcal N(0,Q),\quad v_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R),
\end{equation}
$\Phi$ the RK4 step of $\dot x=f(x,u)$ under the zero-order hold, $Q=dt\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_p x_{\rm std})^2$
(Euler--Maruyama increment after the step, $\sigma_p=0.005$), $R=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_yy_{\rm std})^2$
($\sigma_y=0.03$), $w,v$ independent and white. $F_k:=\partial_x\Phi(x_k,u_k)$, $H_k:=\partial_xh(x_k)$.
$D_\sigma=\mathrm{diag}(x_{\rm std})$ is the normalisation of \texttt{metrics.py}; for a matrix $M$ write
$\tilde M:=D_\sigma^{-1}MD_\sigma^{-1}$. An \emph{observer} is any measurable map
$\hat x_k=\psi_k(y_{0:k},u_{0:k})$ (the deployed decoder-side observer, the EKF and the grey-box filter are
such maps; the prior $\hat x_0$ is a constant). Errors are $e_k=\hat x_k-x_k$; the paper's per-state NRMSE on the
settled window $S$ is a per-episode RMS of $e_{k,j}/\sigma_j$ averaged over episodes, and the quantity a Cram\'er--Rao
bound controls is the \emph{pooled} RMS $\big(\tfrac1{|S|}\sum_{k\in S}\mathbb E[e_{k,j}^2]\big)^{1/2}/\sigma_j$, which
is never smaller than the mean of per-episode RMS values (Jensen); every comparison below uses the pooled form.
$\Sigma_x$ is the \emph{climatology}: the covariance of the settled state under the evaluation inputs, the
reference against which NRMSE~$=1$ means ``no information''.

\begin{definition}[information matrices along a window; information budget]\label{def:m4-budget}
Fix an episode distribution (initial condition, input record, noises) and a window length $W$ (samples).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Local recursion} (along one realised trajectory): $P_{k-W}=\Sigma_x$ and, for $i=k-W,\dots,k-1$,
$P^-_{i+1}=F_iP_iF_i^\top+Q$, $P_{i+1}=\big((P^-_{i+1})^{-1}+H_{i+1}^\top R^{-1}H_{i+1}\big)^{-1}$ (after the update
with $y_{k-W}$ at the start). $P_k(W)$ is the Kalman covariance of the linearised plant.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Bayesian recursion} \citep{tichavsky1998posterior}: $J_{k-W}=\Sigma_x^{-1}+\mathbb E[H^\top R^{-1}H]$
and
\begin{gather*}
J_{i+1}=D^{22}_i-(D^{12}_i)^\top(J_i+D^{11}_i)^{-1}D^{12}_i,\\
D^{11}_i=\mathbb E[F_i^\top Q^{-1}F_i],\qquad D^{12}_i=-\mathbb E[F_i^\top]Q^{-1},\qquad D^{22}_i=Q^{-1}+\mathbb E[H_{i+1}^\top R^{-1}H_{i+1}],
\end{gather*}
the expectations over the episode distribution (estimated as averages over the evaluation episodes). Write
$P^{\rm B}_k(W):=J_k^{-1}$. $W=\infty$ (``full past'') starts the recursion at $k=0$.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Budget and bounds.} $\mathrm{bits}_k(W):=\tfrac12\log_2\det\tilde\Sigma_x-\tfrac12\log_2\det\tilde P^{\rm B}_k(W)$
(information about $x_k$ delivered by the last $W$ samples of $(y,u)$ beyond climatology);
$\beta_j(W):=\big(\mathrm{mean}_{k\in S}[\tilde P^{\rm B}_k(W)]_{jj}\big)^{1/2}$ (per-state floor);
$\beta_{\rm unm}(W)$ its mean over unmeasured states; $\rho(W):=\beta_{\rm unm}(W)/\beta_{\rm unm}(\infty)$ (window deficit).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Fisher information is the observability Gramian]\label{prop:m4-gramian}
[proven] (a) For a linear plant $x_{k+1}=F_dx_k+G_du_k$, $y_k=Cx_k+v_k$ without process noise, the Fisher
information about $x_k$ carried by $y_{k-W:k}$ (given $u$) is
\[
\mathcal I_k(W)=\sum_{i=0}^{W}\Phi_i^\top C^\top R^{-1}C\,\Phi_i,\qquad \Phi_i=\partial x_{k-i}/\partial x_k=F_d^{-i},
\]
the backward observability Gramian of the window weighted by $R^{-1}$ (for $R=\sigma^2I$: the Gramian over
$\sigma^2$), and $J_k(W)=\Sigma_x^{-1}+\mathcal I_k(W)$ is the $Q\to0$ limit of both recursions of
Definition~\ref{def:m4-budget}. It does not depend on $u$. (b) With process noise the two recursions coincide and
equal the Kalman filter's posterior covariance (information form of the Riccati recursion). (c) For the nonlinear
plant \eqref{eq:m4-plant} the Bayesian recursion is the exact Fisher information of the joint density of
$(x_{0:k},y_{0:k})$ given $u$ \citep[Prop.~1]{tichavsky1998posterior}: $-\mathbb E[\nabla^2_{x_k,x_{k+1}}\log p(x_{k+1}|x_k,u_k)]$
has the blocks $D^{11},D^{12},D^{22}-Q^{-1}$ exactly, because the transition noise is additive Gaussian, and
$-\mathbb E[\nabla^2\log p(y|x)]=\mathbb E[H^\top R^{-1}H]$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Intuition.} Each measurement in the window sees the current state through the backward flow; the Gramian
adds up how far the window's outputs move when the current state moves, in units of the noise.
\begin{proof}
(a) $y_{k-i}=C F_d^{-i}x_k+(\text{known function of }u)+v_{k-i}$, a Gaussian linear model in $x_k$ with design
matrix $CF_d^{-i}$; the Fisher information of independent Gaussian observations adds. The $Q\to0$ limit of (i):
with $Q=0$ the covariance recursion is the information recursion $J\mapsto F^{-\top}JF^{-1}+H^\top R^{-1}H$ in
the frame of $x_k$ (check [2]). (b) Linear plant: $F,H$ are constant, the expectations in (ii) are trivial, and
the block elimination $D^{22}-D^{12\top}(J+D^{11})^{-1}D^{12}$ is the matrix-inversion-lemma form of the Riccati
update. (c) Differentiate $\log p(x_{k+1}|x_k)=-\tfrac12\|x_{k+1}-\Phi(x_k,u_k)\|^2_{Q^{-1}}+c$ twice; the term
$\mathbb E[(x_{k+1}-\Phi)^\top Q^{-1}\partial^2_{x}\Phi]$ vanishes because the residual is the zero-mean noise,
independent of $x_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[what any observer must pay]\label{thm:m4-bounds}
[(a), (b), (c) proven for the linear-Gaussian plant, where (a) is attained; (a) proven for the nonlinear plant
\eqref{eq:m4-plant} in its Bayesian form (Gaussian noises, $\Phi,h\in C^1$, a prior density that is $C^1$ and
vanishes at the boundary of its support; the uniform initial box violates the last condition at $k=0$, so the
recursion is started from the smooth density of $x_1$, which changes nothing on the settled window); the
\emph{numbers} for nonlinear plants are Monte Carlo estimates of the expectations in Definition~\ref{def:m4-budget}(ii)]
For every observer $\psi$ and every $k$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (\emph{Bayesian Cram\'er--Rao / van Trees}) $\mathbb E[e_ke_k^\top]\succeq J_k^{-1}=P^{\rm B}_k(W)$ when
$\psi_k$ uses the last $W$ samples only, and $\succeq P^{\rm B}_k(\infty)$ for any $\psi_k$. Hence the pooled
NRMSE of state $j$ is at least $\beta_j(W)$, resp.\ $\beta_j(\infty)$, and the pooled total NRMSE at least
$\big(\tfrac1n\sum_j\beta_j^2\big)^{1/2}$. For the linear-Gaussian plant the Kalman filter attains every one of
these with equality.
\item[(b)] (\emph{mutual information}) For a linear plant and any prior of covariance $\Sigma_x$,
$I(x_k;y_{0:k}\,|\,u_{0:k})\le\mathrm{bits}_k(\infty)=\tfrac12\log_2\det\big(I+\tilde\Sigma_x\tilde{\mathcal I}_k\big)$,
with equality for a Gaussian prior; the same with $W$ in place of $\infty$ for the window's information.
\item[(c)] (\emph{rate--distortion: the guarantee in bits}) Let $D:=\tfrac1n\,\mathrm{mean}_{k\in S}\,\mathbb E\|D_\sigma^{-1}e_k\|^2$
be the normalised total MSE of an observer that extracts $I$ bits about $x_k$ from the window
($I\le\mathrm{bits}_k(W)$ by (b) and the data-processing inequality, since $\hat x_k$ is a function of the window).
For a Gaussian prior
\[
D\ \ge\ (\det\tilde\Sigma_x)^{1/n}\,2^{-2I/n}\ \ge\ (\det\tilde\Sigma_x)^{1/n}\,2^{-2\,\mathrm{bits}_k(W)/n};
\]
equivalently, reaching a per-state RMS of $\sqrt D$ \emph{requires} at least
$I^*(D)=\tfrac n2\log_2\big((\det\tilde\Sigma_x)^{1/n}/D\big)$ bits per window, and an observer whose window
delivers fewer bits fails to reach $D$, whatever its architecture. For a non-Gaussian prior replace
$\tfrac12\log_2\big((2\pi e)^n\det\tilde\Sigma_x\big)$ by the differential entropy $h(\tilde x_k)$ in the Shannon
lower bound $R(D)\ge h(\tilde x_k)-\tfrac n2\log_2(2\pi eD)$. The two bounds are ordered: the rate--distortion
floor never exceeds the Cram\'er--Rao total (arithmetic--geometric mean inequality on the eigenvalues of
$\tilde P^{\rm B}_k$), so (a) is the sharper statement per state and (c) is the same statement in bits.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Intuition.} The window is a noisy channel from the current state to the observer; (a) says how precisely
the state can be read off it in each direction, (c) counts the bits that reading requires. Both depend only on
the plant, the sensors, the noise, the window and the input record, never on the observer.
\begin{proof}
(a) Van Trees' inequality \citep[Ch.~2.4]{vantrees1968detection}: for a prior with the stated regularity and any
estimator, $\mathbb E[ee^\top]\succeq J_B^{-1}$ with $J_B$ the Bayesian information of $x_k$; the recursive form of
$J_B$ for the Markov model \eqref{eq:m4-plant} is Theorem~1 of \citet{tichavsky1998posterior}, whose blocks are
those of Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-gramian}(c); restricting the observer to the window is the same statement with
the prior placed at $k-W$. Attainment: for the linear-Gaussian plant the posterior is Gaussian with covariance
$P_k$ (the Kalman recursion), the conditional mean has exactly this error covariance, and $P_k=J_k^{-1}$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-gramian}(b). (b) For a linear plant $y_{0:k}=\mathcal L x_k+\mathcal N$ with $\mathcal N$
Gaussian and independent of $x_k$ (process noise, sensor noise, and the known input contribute a deterministic
offset): among all inputs of covariance $\Sigma_x$ the Gaussian maximises the mutual information of an additive
Gaussian channel \citep[Thm.~9.6.5]{cover2006elements}, and for a Gaussian prior
$I=\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_x-\tfrac12\log\det P_k$. (c) The Shannon lower bound \citep[Thm.~10.4.1 and
eq.~(10.74)]{cover2006elements}: $R(D)\ge h(\tilde x)-\tfrac n2\log_2(2\pi eD)$ for squared error $nD$; with the
Gaussian entropy, $R(D)\ge\tfrac12\log_2\big(\det\tilde\Sigma_x/D^n\big)$, and $R(D)\le I$ because the observer's
output achieves distortion $D$ with $I$ bits of information about $x_k$ (rate--distortion converse). Solve for
$D$. Ordering: $\tfrac1n\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde P\ge(\det\tilde P)^{1/n}=(\det\tilde\Sigma_x)^{1/n}2^{-2\,\mathrm{bits}/n}$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{What is a guarantee here and what is not.} For a linear plant with Gaussian noises (a)--(c) are
theorems with the constants of the data generator, and (a) is tight. For the nonlinear benchmarks (a) in its
Bayesian form remains a theorem, but its expectations are Monte Carlo averages over the evaluation episodes, so
the quoted floors carry sampling error (a few per cent on the slow states, check [7]). The local recursion (i) is
the EKF's own covariance and is \emph{not} a bound for nonlinear plants; it is reported next to the Bayesian one
because it is what an engineer computes and because the two differ exactly where the Bayesian bound is loose: by
Jensen's inequality averaging information before inverting lowers the floor, so on the chaotic plants the Bayesian
floor is far below anything achievable (lorenz $0.0014$ against the exact-model EKF's $0.0064$; r\"ossler $0.0014$
against $0.048$ for the local recursion), while on the near-linear plants the two coincide (quad\_tank $0.0198$ both;
Table~\ref{tab:m4-budgets}). Nothing in this section bounds a learned observer \emph{from above}.

\begin{proposition}[excitation and the budget]\label{prop:m4-excitation}
[(a), (b) proven; (c) proven; (d) proven (data processing) with an empirical reading]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Linear plants: the input cannot buy information about the state.} $J_k(W)$ does not depend on
$u$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-gramian}(a,b)): the per-state floors $\beta_j(W)$ are the same for every input
record. Excitation changes the climatology $\Sigma_x$, hence $\mathrm{bits}_k(W)$ \emph{relative to climatology},
but not the absolute error floor. (This is the estimation-side counterpart of the identification-side fact that
persistent excitation is needed to learn the \emph{model}, \citealp[Ch.~13]{ljung1999system}: with a known model
the state information is a Gramian property.)
\item[(b)] \emph{Nonlinear plants.} $u$ enters $J_k(W)$ only through the operating points $(x_i,u_i)$ at which
$F_i,H_i$ are evaluated; the budget moves with the evaluation inputs exactly as much as the linearisation does.
On quad\_tank (square-root orifice law) the full-past floor changes by less than $1\%$ when the input time scale is
varied by a factor $16$ (check [4]; $\mathrm{bits}=21.27$ at every level of the dose-response experiment).
\item[(c)] \emph{Short windows starve slow modes.} $J_k(W)$ is nondecreasing in $W$ and converges to the
steady-state information, so $\rho(W)\ge1$ decreases to $1$. For an unmeasured state that reaches the output only
through a mode of rate $\lambda_s$, the window information about it keeps growing until $W\approx1/\lambda_s$: a
window short on the scale $1/\lambda_s$ is a deficit $\rho(W)\gg1$ no matter how rich the input. quad\_tank
($\lambda_s^{-1}=84$~s, $\alpha=0.1$): $\beta_{\rm unm}=0.052,0.030,0.020$ at $W=1/\alpha,3/\alpha,\infty$
($\rho(3/\alpha)=1.50$); battery (SOC is an integrator): $0.033,0.0084,0.0036$ ($\rho=2.3$); vdp: $0.0051,0.0038,0.0038$
($\rho=1.0$).
\item[(d)] \emph{What the latent can carry.} $z_k$ and $c_k$ are functions of $(y_{0:k},u_{0:k})$, so
$I(x_k;z_k,c_k)\le I(x_k;y_{0:k},u_{0:k})\le\mathrm{bits}_k(\infty)$ and every decoder-side observer obeys
Theorem~\ref{thm:m4-bounds} with the full-past floor. By Lemma~\ref{lem:m2-tail} the latent's dependence on
measurements older than $W$ is attenuated by $e^{-\alpha W}$, and the context carries $u$ only; the \emph{reading}
(not a theorem) is that a decoder-side observer with filter rate $\alpha$ is, in the information sense, a window
observer with $W$ of a few $1/\alpha$, so its floor is $\beta_j(W)$ with $W\in[1/\alpha,3/\alpha]$ rather than
$\beta_j(\infty)$. On quad\_tank HyperKKL's unmeasured error ($0.067$ id, $0.093$ sequence OOD) lies above
$\beta_{\rm unm}(1/\alpha)=0.052$, i.e.\ $2.2\times$ the $3/\alpha$ floor and $3.4\times$ the full-past floor, while the
grey-box EKF ($0.0196$) is \emph{at} the full-past floor ($0.0198$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a), (b) are read off Definition~\ref{def:m4-budget} and Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-gramian}. (c) Monotonicity:
adding measurements adds a positive semidefinite term and the Bayesian recursion is monotone in $J$
\citep{tichavsky1998posterior}; the convergence is that of the Riccati recursion. (d) Data processing inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the learning side: a necessary condition for any learned decoder]\label{prop:m4-learning}
[proven, elementary; the sample-complexity statement is the well-specified linear case] Let the decoder be fitted by
empirical risk minimisation on training triples $(z',c',x')$ with regressor features $\varphi(z',c')\in\mathbb R^p$
(for a network, the features of its last layer; for the grey-box filter, the inputs of its residual network) and
training covariance $C_\varphi=\mathbb E_{\rm train}[\varphi\varphi^\top]$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (\emph{rank / persistent excitation}) If $C_\varphi$ has a null space $\mathcal N\ne\{0\}$, two
hypotheses that differ only by a linear functional supported on $\mathcal N$ have the same empirical risk for every
sample size, so at a query with $\varphi\notin\mathcal N^\perp$ the learned map is undetermined by the data: its
error there is at least half the diameter of the hypotheses compatible with the training set, independent of $N$.
The condition that excludes this uniformly in time, $\inf_t\lambda_{\min}\big(\tfrac1W\sum_{i=t-W}^t\varphi_i\varphi_i^\top\big)\ge c>0$,
is persistent excitation of the \emph{regressors}; it is a property of the training input distribution and of
the plant together, not of the evaluation data.
\item[(b)] (\emph{how many informative samples}) For a well-specified linear-in-$\varphi$ map with $p$ parameters
and label noise $\sigma_\ell^2$, the excess risk of least squares is $\sigma_\ell^2\,\mathrm{tr}\,(N C_\varphi)^{-1}\approx\sigma_\ell^2p/N$
\citep[Ch.~3]{hastie2009elements}; the same excess therefore needs $N\propto p$. A grey-box filter that knows the
mass balance and learns only a low-dimensional residual ($p_g$) reaches a given excess with $N$ smaller by the
factor $p_g/p_f$ than a generic decoder ($p_f$), and the directions it does \emph{not} learn (the known structure)
have zero excess at every $N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The empirical risk depends on the hypothesis only through its values at the training regressors, which are
$\mathcal N^\perp$-measurable; two hypotheses differing on $\mathcal N$ agree there. The lower bound is the
two-point argument of Corollary~\ref{cor:m1-ood}(b). (b) $\hat w-w=(\varphi^\top\varphi)^{-1}\varphi^\top\epsilon$
has covariance $\sigma_\ell^2(\varphi^\top\varphi)^{-1}$; take the trace and the law of large numbers
$\varphi^\top\varphi\approx NC_\varphi$ (check [6]: ratios $0.84$--$1.49$ at $p=2$ and $p=64$).
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Honest scope.} (a) is necessary, not sufficient; (b) is the linear well-specified rate, and for the
deep decoders of this paper only the direction of the statement is claimed (fewer effective parameters, fewer
informative samples). The two sides are distinct: Theorem~\ref{thm:m4-bounds} is about the \emph{evaluation}
episodes (what any observer can extract from them) and is unchanged by any amount of training data;
Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-learning} is about the \emph{training} episodes and is unchanged by the evaluation
sensor. An observer can fail for either reason, and the two have different remedies (a longer window or a better
sensor against the first; richer or structured training against the second).

\begin{remark}[Kolmogorov complexity: why it cannot give the number, and what it does say]\label{rem:m4-kolmogorov}
The Kolmogorov complexity $K(s)$ of a string (the length of the shortest program producing it) is not computable:
no algorithm outputs $K(s)$ for every $s$, and every computable lower bound of $K$ is bounded above
\citep[Thm.~2.3.2 and its corollaries]{li2008kolmogorov}; it is also defined only up to an additive constant that
depends on the reference machine. A ``guarantee'' of the form ``the observer fails because $K(\text{data})$ is too
small'' is therefore not a statement one can evaluate on quad\_tank, and the per-system numbers of
Table~\ref{tab:m4-budgets} are Shannon and Fisher quantities, which are computable from the model and the noise.
The honest connection is through the minimum description length principle \citep{rissanen1978modeling,grunwald2007mdl}:
a two-part code pays $L(\text{model})+L(\text{data}\,|\,\text{model})$ bits. The grey-box filter's first term is the
few parameters of its residual network (the mass balance costs nothing, it is given); a generic decoder's first
term is its weight description. The second term, the bits needed to encode the residual error of the state at the
observer's precision, is bounded below by $I^*(D)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:m4-bounds}(c): no coding scheme describes
the state to within $D$ with fewer bits than the window carries. MDL thus \emph{interprets}
Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-learning}(b) (fewer model bits, fewer samples to pay them) and reproduces
Theorem~\ref{thm:m4-bounds}(c) in its data term; it adds no quantity beyond them, and the Kolmogorov version of
the same two-part argument \citep[Ch.~5]{li2008kolmogorov} adds only uncomputability. We therefore make no
Kolmogorov claim.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{What it predicts, and how it is tested.} Pre-registered in \texttt{experiments/information/PREREG.md}
(thresholds fixed from the budgets before any comparison with achieved errors), scored mechanically by
\texttt{score.py} into \texttt{RESULTS.md}; the test plants' partial-knowledge runs (TEST-C3) use coloured Student-$t$
noise on a $30\%$-perturbed plant, and the post hoc recomputation of their budgets under that model
(\texttt{BUDGET\_FIX.md}) moves $\beta_{\rm unm}(3/\alpha)$ to $0.065$ (cartpole), $0.097$ (highway\_traffic) and $0.020$
(two\_link\_arm, now marginal) without changing any group of Table~\ref{tab:m4-budgets} or any verdict below; the training-distribution budgets equal the evaluation ones to within
$0.3$~bits on every plant, so the \texttt{id} column stands for both.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P0)] \emph{Guarantee.} Every achieved pooled RMS, per state and in total, of every method in every stored
run (448 method--tier cells, $12$ plants, exact and partial knowledge) must lie above $0.95\,\beta_j(\infty)$.
\textbf{FAIL by the letter}: $3$ cells are below, all of them the exact-model EKF on slow states (quad\_tank $x_3$
on sequence OOD, $0.0233$ vs $0.0245$; cartpole $x_2$, $0.0134$ vs $0.0145$, and its total), and in all three the
floor lies inside the bootstrap $95\%$ interval of the achieved pooled RMS (per-episode coefficient of variation
$0.3$--$0.5$: a slow error process yields few independent samples per episode). No learned observer and no
grey-box filter is below any floor. The $5\%$ tolerance was too strict for slow states; the post-hoc reading is
``no floor exceeds the achieved error by more than its sampling uncertainty''.
\emph{Tightness}: the exact-model EKF sits at the floor on the near-linear plants (ratio $0.95$--$1.07$ on
quad\_tank, cartpole, bioreactor, duffing, highway\_traffic, fhn) and above it where the Bayesian floor is loose
(lorenz $4.6$, r\"ossler $30$) or the EKF is not the optimal filter (battery $2.0$, vdp $1.7$, two\_link\_arm $1.9$).
\item[(P1)] \emph{Rank correlation across plants.} HyperKKL's unmeasured NRMSE against $\beta_{\rm unm}(3/\alpha)$:
Spearman $\rho_s=0.65$ ($p=0.02$, $n=12$; $0.76$ on sequence OOD) -- \textbf{PASS} (rule $\ge0.5$). Exact-model EKF
against $\beta_{\rm unm}(\infty)$: $0.58$ -- \textbf{FAIL} (rule $\ge0.7$; the EKF is far from the Bayesian floor on
the chaotic plants, where that floor is loose). Best grey-box against $\beta_{\rm unm}(\infty)$: $-0.05$ --
\textbf{FAIL}: on bioreactor, lorenz, r\"ossler and two\_link\_arm the grey-box filter fails ($0.24$--$0.66$ NRMSE,
divergence and model error), so its error is not information-limited; the floor binds only an estimator that is
close to optimal.
\item[(P2)] \emph{Where learned observers lose.} The gap (HyperKKL $-$ best grey-box)/grey-box on the unmeasured
states, partial-knowledge runs: median $+464\%$ in the window-starved group against $-66\%$ in the rich group on
sequence OOD ($+321\%$ vs $-63\%$ on \texttt{id}), with $5/5$ window-starved plants above the rich median --
\textbf{PASS} on both tiers. The exception inside the rich group is fhn ($+533\%$), which the budget does not
explain; two\_link\_arm (absolutely starved) has a small gap ($+19\%$) because both observers are poor there.
\item[(P3)] \emph{Dose--response on the evaluation inputs} (quad\_tank, vdp, two\_link\_arm; input time scale
$\times\{\tfrac14,\tfrac12,1,2,4\}$ within the same amplitude box; stored models, no retraining;
\texttt{dose\_response.py}). Predicted: the budget is flat on quad\_tank (Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-excitation}(b)),
every method stays above the floor at every level, the exact-model EKF's error tracks the budget where it moves
and stays flat where it does not, and HyperKKL's error moves more than the EKF's (a learning-side, not an
information effect). \emph{Found} ($3$ seeds, $32$ episodes per level): the budget is flat on all three plants
(full-past floor max/min $1.000$, $1.05$, $1.14$) and so is the exact-model EKF ($1.06$, $1.09$, $1.16$) -- P3b
\textbf{PASS} $3/3$; every method stays above $0.95\,\beta_j(\infty)$ on vdp and two\_link\_arm, while on quad\_tank
$5$ of $30$ cells (exact-model EKF and grey-box, $x_3$/$x_4$) fall $5$--$7\%$ below it, once more within the sampling
error of a slow state at $32$ episodes -- P3a \textbf{PASS} $2/3$ by the letter; the grey-box filter sits at
$0.95$--$1.01$ of the full-past floor at every level. P3c \textbf{FAIL}: HyperKKL's error varies by only
$1.08$--$1.32\times$ across the levels, less than $1.5\times$ the EKF's variation, and on quad\_tank it \emph{falls}
monotonically with slower inputs ($0.076\to0.058$) while the budget does not move: a small learning-side
(time-scale shift) effect, not an information effect, and smaller than predicted. HyperKKL stays at
$1.9$--$2.6\times\beta_{\rm unm}(3/\alpha)$ and $2.9$--$3.8\times\beta_{\rm unm}(\infty)$ at every level (P3d).
On two\_link\_arm every observer, the exact-model EKF included ($0.38$--$0.45$), is $15$--$20\times$ above the floor
($0.021$--$0.024$): the floor is loose there (strong nonlinearity, Jensen) and the EKF is far from optimal; the
plant is poorly observable in practice, not information-starved in the Bayesian sense.
\item[(P4)] \emph{Prediction for the quad\_tank training-enrichment experiment} (\texttt{experiments/quadtank}).
The arms change the training inputs only; the evaluation episodes, hence $\beta$, are unchanged:
$\beta_{\rm unm}=0.0198$ (full past; $x_3$ $0.0245$, $x_4$ $0.0151$), $0.0201$ ($10/\alpha$), $0.0297$ ($3/\alpha$),
$0.0517$ ($1/\alpha$). The stored grey-box EKF ($0.0185$--$0.0197$) is within $1\%$ of the full-past floor: it is
information-optimal on quad\_tank, and no observer of any kind can beat it by more than that. Prediction: no arm
brings HyperKKL's unmeasured error below $0.020$; if the fading-memory window is the binding constraint, not
below $0.030$; the gap that training enrichment can close is from the present $0.065$--$0.095$ to the
$0.03$--$0.05$ band at most, and the rest is the window deficit, which only a longer memory removes. The enriched
inputs add no bits to the evaluation budget by construction (Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-excitation}(a,b));
they can only change the training coverage of Proposition~\ref{prop:m4-learning}.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Real battery} (\texttt{real\_battery.py}, Panasonic 18650PF, identified 2-RC model driven by the measured
current, 30-min windows with the prior $20\%$ off, settled half, as in the real-data evaluation; the real
sensor noise is not identified, so two settings are reported). With the tuned EKF's own $(Q,R)$ ($R_{V_m}=0.1$~V,
which absorbs model error) the local SOC floor is $0.70$--$1.09\%$ SOC (US06, HWFET, NN; Bayesian $0.13\%$);
with sensor-level noise ($R_{V_m}=3$~mV) it is $0.07$--$0.11\%$ ($0.02\%$). The achieved settled SOC RMSE is
HyperKKL $2.18/0.91/0.73\%$, grey-box UKF $0.87/0.94/1.25\%$, EKF $8.85/1.19/1.95\%$ (\texttt{tab\_real}): every
observer is $7$--$100\times$ above the sensor-level floor and the best ones sit at the level of the tuned filter's
own covariance. On the real cell the error is model error (the identified ECM), not a shortage of information;
the floor is a statement about the model and is reported, not used as a guarantee.
